# Business planning module: the product-director lens

Loaded second in the domain-pass order, after Product. Product decides the problem, the primary user, and the requirements; this module decides who pays and for what, how a sale becomes access, what the customer lifecycle does to money, access, and data, how each number the team decides with is computed, how changes reach and leave customers, and how customers are heard.

Applicability: applicable when any of these holds: the plan charges money or displays a price; people outside the team create accounts or sign up; the product sends usage telemetry about people outside the team; or a contact-sales, waitlist, or feedback surface collects customer signals. Typical cases are a saas-dashboard, a mobile-app or extension with accounts or purchases, a sold game, a marketing-site with pricing or contact-sales, and an api-service or library with a hosted tier, a paid license, telemetry, or sign-up. Installing a free package or app is not an account. A project none of these signals reaches excludes the domain `by-design:` with a `revisit when:` predicate such as "any task adds a price, a checkout, a sign-up route reachable without an invite, an analytics SDK, or a feedback destination". An undecided revenue model is `unknown` and never licenses the exclusion. The domain is never deferred: the paying unit, the entitlement scope, and the billing system of record shape the data model.

The `monetized` overlay forbids excluding this domain. It fires when the request, an interview answer, or decision 2 charges money, sells plans or seats, or meters paid usage; a Phase 4 decision that starts charging adds `monetized` to the frontmatter overlays list and to the `- Overlays:` line of the `### Archetype confidence` block before emission, then rechecks the business matrix row. The overlay acts on the matrix row only; the floor in the Self-audit rubric is what keeps billing safety from dropping by scale.

Conditional requirements: a requirement whose criterion opens with IF binds only while its condition holds. Drop it through the layer that removed the condition: `archetype` when a plan decision removed it, citing the decision, for example `dropped-by archetype R-BIZ-11, R-BIZ-12 (D2: no charge in this plan)`; `scale` when the condition is a scale; `form` when the product form has no such surface. Cite several decisions with commas, never semicolons, because the validator splits disposition clauses on `;`. An unknown condition is never a reason to drop; it is an Open Question with a default.

Scale calibration: scale sets how much of this module binds, never whether a plan that charges protects the customer's money, access, and data. Weekend: R-BIZ-2 is one paragraph; every other requirement whose condition holds lands as acceptance lines on existing tasks, and the scale layer drops only the requirements whose condition is scale-bound, for example `dropped-by scale R-BIZ-1, R-BIZ-3, R-BIZ-4, R-BIZ-25 (weekend: success is the R-PRD-5 metrics alone)`. Analytics, metric, flag, and deprecation requirements (R-BIZ-20 to R-BIZ-24) drop through the archetype layer citing decision 6 or 9 when their conditions do not hold, never by scale. Side-project: R-BIZ-1, R-BIZ-3, and R-BIZ-4 land in the reduced forms their bodies name, R-BIZ-25 drops by scale, and R-BIZ-23 drops through the archetype layer citing decision 9 when no task introduces a flag. At weekend and side-project scale R-BIZ-19 drops by scale only when no invoice-term tier, custom deal, admin tooling, or SLA service credit is planned; otherwise its override records and the admin actions those features need land. Funded-product and enterprise: every requirement whose condition holds lands in full. At any scale, a plan that charges never drops R-BIZ-7 to R-BIZ-16 through the scale layer; at weekend scale they fold into at most two tasks (catalog and checkout; webhook and lifecycle). A plan whose appetite cannot hold them is mis-scaled: the scale changes, not the requirement, just as a weekend project with passwords still hashes them with argon2id.

## Lineage

Descends from productauditor (hannsxpeter/auditor-suite, skills/productauditor), which audits a product "through a product manager's and head of product's lens" in nine dimensions; its 58 cards become the requirement groups below: CLM claims and delivery, ENT plans and entitlements, BILL billing lifecycle, CUST accounts and operations, MET instrumentation, KPI metric definitions, SHIP release and sunset, EXP experiments, and VOC feedback. The plan designs out the five classes productauditor treats as Critical: charging more than, other than, or after what the customer chose; refusing what a plan includes; destroying customer data in a lifecycle event without notice or a recovery window; a business model nothing enforces (sandbox checkout in production, access from the success redirect, an unknown plan falling through to paid, a trial nothing ends); and selling something that does not exist or is invented.

R-BIZ-1, R-BIZ-2, and R-BIZ-4 have no auditor counterpart, by design: productauditor files no strategy, market, pricing-level, or roadmap opinion and writes "Target customer: not stated" rather than invent one. An audit cannot judge the bet; a plan can decide it, and a later productaudit then has a promise inventory, a plan catalog, and a metric register to read. R-BIZ-3's register also inverts MET-R7 (a named metric nothing measures). Sources: the North Star Framework (Amplitude North Star Playbook); Ramanujam and Tacke, Monetizing Innovation (2016); Croll and Yoskovitz, Lean Analytics (2013); Kohavi, Tang, and Xu, Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments (2020); Torres, Continuous Discovery Habits (2021); Hodgson, "Feature Toggles" (martinfowler.com, 2017); RFC 9745 (Deprecation header); RFC 8594 (Sunset header).

Dated sources, from the productauditor facts file in hannsxpeter/auditor-suite (reviewed 2026-10-01; recheck any fact older than six months before a plan cites it): ROSCA, 15 U.S.C. 8403, requires a simple mechanism to stop recurring charges. The California Automatic Renewal Law (Business and Professions Code 17600 and following, as amended by AB 2863, for contracts entered, amended, or extended on or after 1 July 2025, 17602(j)) sets express-consent and consent-record, same-medium cancellation, annual-reminder, price-change (notice 7 to 30 days before), and free-trial (trials over 31 days, notice 3 to 21 days before the end) duties. The FTC Negative Option ("click-to-cancel") Rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on 8 July 2025; an advance notice of proposed rulemaking of 13 March 2026 had produced no proposed or final rule at review, and the rule is never cited as law. The New York City subscription-cancellation rule applies from 1 October 2026. The EU Data Act (Regulation (EU) 2023/2854) Chapter VI governs switching and data export for software services from 12 September 2025. A legal window enters a plan only through a cited source or an Open Question; godplans names the signal and the regime it may trigger and never asserts that a regime applies.

Cited, not restated. Product: problem, user, requirements, NFRs, success targets, and the statement of which regulations apply (R-PRD-1 to R-PRD-7). UX: the activation event and its step budget (R-UX-14), fee, total, and cancellation-effort disclosure (R-UX-17), destructive confirmations (R-UX-5). Launch: positioning, landing, conversion waterfall, UTM values, waitlist double opt-in (R-LAUNCH-1, R-LAUNCH-4, R-LAUNCH-12, R-LAUNCH-16, R-LAUNCH-17), fed by this module's segment and catalog. Security: roles, tenancy, request-settable privilege fields, webhook signatures and retired mounts, abuse limits, erasure, and consent before trackers (R-SEC-3, R-SEC-4, R-SEC-16, R-SEC-19, R-SEC-21, R-SEC-29); "does this plan include this feature" stays here. Database: money types, delete behavior, idempotency, outbox, and amount reconciliation (R-DB-4, R-DB-6, R-DB-7, R-DB-9, R-DB-15, R-DB-23); subscription-state reconciliation stays here. Architecture: bounded contexts, entity ownership, API versioning (R-ARCH-5, R-ARCH-7, R-ARCH-20), fed by decisions 1 and 4. Code quality: read sites for stored gating values and lifecycle transitions (R-CODE-23). Deploy: canary, flag defaults per rung, flag-name lineage, rollback rehearsal (R-DEPLOY-7, R-DEPLOY-11, R-DEPLOY-14, R-DEPLOY-16). Observe: SLOs and alerts; product analytics and business KPIs, which R-OBS-20 excludes, live here. Repo: the developer changelog, SemVer, and generated platform release notes (R-REPO-8, R-REPO-13). LLM: model spend caps and output feedback (R-LLM-15, R-LLM-19); model-call quotas a plan sells are R-BIZ-10 quotas. Stack: the payments dimension (R-STACK-4) records decision 4. Build: the hollow-data grep (R-BUILD-16).

## Decisions to force

Hardest to reverse first. Each lands in the plan's Decisions section as a grounded decision with a Falsifier block, a flagged hypothesis with a validation task, or an Open Question with a recommended default. When decision 2 is no charge, decisions 3, 4, and 7 and the payment transitions of decision 5 are not forced; they live in decision 2's `revisit when:` predicate. At weekend and side-project scale, decisions 9 and 11 take their scale defaults as one line each inside decision 2's entry.

1. Paying unit and customer of record.
   - Question: who holds the subscription, the entitlements, and the data: a workspace or organization, an individual user, or both?
   - Why hard to reverse: it fixes entitlement scope, seat billing, the analytics group id, shared-work ownership, and the tenancy key; moving subscriptions from users to workspaces later is a data, billing, and customer-communication migration at once.
   - Options: (a) the workspace holds subscription, entitlements, and shared content; (b) the user holds them; (c) both, with a written precedence rule and a test.
   - Default: (a), with a workspace of one created at sign-up, whenever the buyer is a business or any roadmap horizon names sharing, seats, or a team plan; (b) only when the buyer is an individual consumer and no horizon names sharing. A product that fits neither is an Open Question that blocks build.
2. Revenue model, value metric, and motion.
   - Question: does this plan charge, for what unit of value, and through which motion?
   - Why hard to reverse: meters, seat counts, and quota counters live in the data model and every consuming path.
   - Options: no charge (with a `revisit when:` predicate), flat subscription, per seat, usage-based, base plus usage, one-time license, store in-app purchase, take rate; motion self-serve, sales-assisted, self-serve with a sales-assisted tier above a threshold, or store.
   - Default: no charge when the request names no price and scale is weekend or side-project, with `revisit when: any task adds a price, a checkout, or a paid tier`. Otherwise one value metric the customer can see and predict, and a pricing model that constrains the data model takes a slot in the interview batch. Usage-based pricing only with a server-side meter and visible current-period usage.
3. Free offer and conversion path.
   - Question: what does a prospect get before paying (nothing; a trial of a named plan, with or without a payment method up front; a free plan with catalog limits; a reverse trial that falls to the free plan), and at which fence does the product ask for money?
   - Why hard to reverse: a free plan withdrawn from an installed base needs grandfathering and notice; an auto-converting trial carries renewal-notice duties a no-card trial does not; the fence decides which limit counters ship first.
   - Default: an explicit decision naming the trial plan and its end state; when undecided, an Open Question that blocks build.
4. Billing system of record, merchant of record, and tax.
   - Question: which system is the source of truth for subscription state, and who collects and remits tax?
   - Why hard to reverse: a local subscription model that is not a projection becomes a second truth that drifts, and changing the merchant of record changes invoices, tax registration, and refunds.
   - Options: (a) the provider is the source of truth and the app holds a webhook-written projection plus a reconciliation job; (b) the app owns subscription state (refused); (c) entitlement checks read the provider or its SDK live through a short-lived cache with no local projection, which fits merchant-of-record and store SDKs at small scale. Merchant of record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar) or processor plus tax engine (Stripe with Stripe Tax); store billing for digital goods unlocked in native apps, under the storefront rules in effect.
   - Default: (a) at funded-product scale and above, (c) below it. R-BIZ-9 to R-BIZ-12 name the entitlement source this decision picks: the webhook-written projection under (a), the provider read through its cache under (c). The merchant-of-record choice is an Open Question decided by tax posture unless the user has decided it.
5. Lifecycle end states, refunds, and data on lapse.
   - Question: what happens to access, members, and data at trial end, failed payment, cancellation, a downgrade below current usage or seat count, and account closure, and what does a refund return and revoke?
   - Why hard to reverse: deletion cannot be undone, and the refund policy decides whether cancellation is immediate or at period end.
   - Options per transition: convert, fall to a free plan, or read-only with export; refunds none, prorated on cancel, or full within a window.
   - Default: access until the paid period ends on cancel; over-limit data and members read-only and exportable with an owner notice; deletion only after a sourced window.
6. Event identity and analytics topology.
   - Question: where do outcome and revenue events originate, and which ids tie them to a user and an account?
   - Why hard to reverse: events already sent cannot be re-identified or backfilled.
   - Options: server-side outcome events after commit with a dedup id and client events for intent only (default); client-only outcome or revenue events (refused); warehouse-first modeling from database rows.
   - Default: server-side events identified with the stable internal user id and grouped by the decision 1 paying unit. At weekend and side-project scale, when every R-PRD-5 metric can be computed from rows the app already writes and no task plans client or server analytics events (the R-LAUNCH-17 waterfall included), saved queries over those rows with no event-tracking client; R-BIZ-20 and R-BIZ-21 then drop through the archetype layer citing this decision.
7. Plan catalog source, currencies, and change policy.
   - Question: where are plans, prices, features, limits, coupons, and provider price ids defined, which currencies and regions sell, and what happens to existing customers when terms change?
   - Why hard to reverse: once copy, entitlements, and checkout each hold a plan, every price change ships three diverging edits and old-terms customers cannot be identified.
   - Options: a code-owned catalog that generates pricing copy and entitlement constants and is checked against the provider (default); a provider-owned catalog read live through lookup keys; CMS-authored pricing (refused unless generated from the catalog).
   - Default: legacy plan keys with effective dates for grandfathered customers, and one catalog price per currency sold.
8. North star, inputs, and guardrails.
   - Question: which one number says customers get the core value, which inputs move it, and which guardrail stops a harmful win?
   - Why hard to reverse: it sets what is instrumented first, and redefining it mid-flight destroys the baseline.
   - Options: a count of paying units, or users for consumer products, performing the core value action per window (default); revenue as the north star (refused: revenue is a lagging guardrail); a composite score (refused unless every component has a register entry).
   - Default: revenue and one quality signal as guardrails.
9. Release control and sunset policy.
   - Question: how do unfinished features stay off, how is a feature stopped in production without a deploy, and how are features, plans, and public API versions retired?
   - Why hard to reverse: a flag mechanism threads through every route, and a deprecation promise made without a policy becomes the policy.
   - Options: one typed flag wrapper whose fallback is current production behavior, with kill switches for features that move money, send customer messages, or delete or export customer data (default at funded-product scale and above); environment-variable flags (refused for kill switches, which must be read at runtime); no flag mechanism at weekend or side-project scale unless a task introduces a flag (default there).
   - Default: a deprecation notice period that is cited or an Open Question.
10. The bet, its kill criterion, and principles.
    - Question: what does this plan bet will be true, what observation would prove it wrong and when, who decides, and which principles settle trade-offs before they are argued?
    - Why hard to reverse: sunk cost grows with every phase, and a bet with no kill criterion is never killed.
    - Options: the R-BIZ-1 falsifier (default); no kill criterion (refused).
    - Default: principles written as "X over Y" (R-BIZ-4).
11. Experimentation posture.
    - Question: will this plan run controlled experiments, and on what unit?
    - Why hard to reverse: assignment keyed to the wrong unit invalidates every result collected.
    - Options: none at weekend or side-project scale (default there, dropped by scale); at funded-product or enterprise scale, none until the north star has a measured baseline (default, R-BIZ-25 dropped through the archetype layer citing this decision); after that, flag-based experiments through decision 9, with the randomization unit equal to the paying unit when price or shared flows change.

## Plan requirements

Consumed by the orchestrator's inversion pass; each becomes acceptance criteria on concrete tasks via `Requirements:` lines.

Director lens

1. R-BIZ-1 The bet and its kill criterion. The bet is a `### D<n>` decision of at most two sentences that cites R-PRD-3, R-PRD-4, and R-PRD-12 instead of restating them and names the R-PRD-9 assumption ids it rests on. Its Falsifier carries the kill criterion in the validator's grammar. Signal opens with the register id (R-BIZ-3), never the word metric, and states the minimum exposure (paying units or users reaching the measured step) below which the verdict is extend once. Failure boundary states the numeric threshold, fixed before the first measurement by the user's answer, the decider's decision, a cited source, or an Open Question that blocks ship, plus the decide-by point, anchored to a milestone such as "launch + 60 days" unless a calendar date falls inside the current cycle (R-ROAD-11). Replan action reads "return to planning; <decider> decides to continue, reconsider <the segment or bet> (pivot), or remove <the product or feature> (stop)". A task citing R-BIZ-1 writes this record with `Verdict: pending`; the decider records the verdict at the review the authority map schedules, which at side-project scale is the R-PRD-17 30-day retrospective. No executing agent records a verdict.
   Criterion: WHEN the business section is emitted at side-project scale or above THE PLAN SHALL state the bet as a decision whose Falsifier carries the R-BIZ-1 kill criterion, and SHALL contain a task citing R-BIZ-1 that records it with `Verdict: pending`.
2. R-BIZ-2 Business model, segment, motion, and packaging. The plan states the paying unit (decision 1), buyer and user roles when they differ, the motion, the revenue model, the value metric, and the free offer (decision 3). Each tier names its fence as a catalog field, and copy that sells a plan for teams, agencies, or several locations requires the workspace paying unit. A price appears only when cited, given by the user, decided by the R-BIZ-4 price decider, or flagged as a hypothesis whose validation task (willingness-to-pay interviews with a stated count, a priced landing or checkout smoke test, or recorded sales conversations) precedes the task that makes the price public. A sales-assisted tier states invoice terms (collection method, payment terms, currency), the contract interval, how a signed order becomes an R-BIZ-19 override record, and the role that owns a new account until its R-UX-14 activation event fires; security questionnaires, DPAs, and order-form ownership are owned Open Questions. A native app that unlocks digital features names its purchase path per storefront from a dated store-policy source (App Store Review Guideline 3.1.1, the Google Play Payments policy) or an Open Question. When launch is applicable, R-LAUNCH-1's first sentence names this segment.
   Criterion: WHEN the business section is emitted THE PLAN SHALL state paying unit, roles, motion, revenue model, value metric, and free offer; IF tiers exist THE PLAN SHALL name each tier's fence and SHALL NOT state an unsourced price; IF a tier is sales-assisted THE PLAN SHALL state its invoice terms, contract interval, order-to-override path, and onboarding owner.
3. R-BIZ-3 North star and metric register. One register, the business-owned `design.metrics-register` document, holds every metric the plan names: when business is applicable, the R-PRD-5 instrumentation task and this register are one task citing both requirements, so one file and one task own the metrics; the register the north star, its inputs, guardrails, every R-PRD-5 success metric, and every kill-criterion and experiment metric. SLOs and other promise-compliance metrics stay with observe (R-OBS-20). The north star is one of the R-PRD-5 metrics and counts toward that cap; the other entries sit outside it. Each entry carries numerator, denominator (`none` for a count such as the default north star), the real-customer population filter, window, source event or query, owner, and the GP task that ships it. At funded-product and enterprise scale, and whenever the plan charges, the register holds at least one input and one guardrail; at side-project scale with no charge, the north star and one input suffice. Every next-horizon theme and later-horizon outcome (R-ROAD-11) names the register metric it targets, and phase Must-have KPIs (R-ROAD-14) resolve to register entries.
   Criterion: WHEN the business section is emitted at side-project scale or above THE PLAN SHALL contain one register whose north star is an R-PRD-5 metric; every north-star, input, guardrail, R-PRD-5, kill-criterion, and experiment metric SHALL resolve to exactly one entry with the seven fields, and every next-horizon theme and later-horizon outcome SHALL name a register metric.
4. R-BIZ-4 Principles and commercial decision rights. Each product principle is written "X over Y", where Y is a property a reasonable product would choose, and is cited by at least one Decisions entry whose rejected option embodies Y; a principle that fails either test is cut. The R-ROAD-18 authority map gains one row per commercial action the plan can take, each with exactly one decider on the R-PRD-14 roster: the bet verdict when R-BIZ-1 lands; price and packaging changes, discounts and custom deals, and plan retirement when the plan charges; deprecation dates when R-BIZ-24 lands; the experiment ship call when R-BIZ-25 lands. With one maintainer, one row naming that maintainer for every listed action satisfies it.
   Criterion: WHEN the business section is emitted at side-project scale or above THE PLAN SHALL contain at least one principle phrased "X over Y" and cited by a decision whose rejected option embodies Y, and the authority map SHALL contain a decider row for each commercial action whose condition holds.

Claims and delivery

5. R-BIZ-5 Promise inventory traced to delivery. Claims include pricing and plan feature lists, checkout and upgrade copy, onboarding, transactional and lifecycle email, store listing, help, terms, support promises, and technical guarantees (end-to-end encryption, data region, "no tracking"). Each maps to an implementing task, a vendor capability with the check that confirms it, or a "coming soon" or "beta" label beside the claim. A support channel or response time maps to a staffed owner and covered hours; an uptime SLA maps to an observe SLO at least as strict plus a service-credit admin action (R-BIZ-19). Plan feature lists render from the catalog (R-BIZ-7). Customer-facing output, paid or free, never comes from fixtures, random values, hardcoded literals, or mock clients on a production path, and sample data is labeled sample (R-BUILD-16 owns the hollow-data grep). Help names screens and settings by labels from the UI message catalog, and copy tasks grep pricing, checkout, sign-up, legal, and email templates for placeholder text (lorem ipsum, [Product Name], Your Company, example.com).
   Criterion: WHEN customer-facing copy or a plan feature list is planned THE PLAN SHALL trace each claim to delivery and test that production selects no mock or stub client and that no sold handler throws not-implemented or returns a constant; IF the plan sells paid features the final Verification phase SHALL check that every paid feature is reachable under production configuration.
6. R-BIZ-6 Committed numbers single-sourced. Trial length, retention period, plan limits, refund window, export formats, and data region, wherever copy states them, come from one constant or catalog field that copy and code both read, with the value from R-PRD-7 or a cited source.
   Criterion: IF customer-facing copy states a committed number THE PLAN SHALL name the one constant or catalog field copy and code both read, and IF a deletion job is planned THE PLAN SHALL test it against the published retention period.

Plans, pricing, and entitlements

7. R-BIZ-7 One plan catalog, coupons, and legacy terms. One catalog module defines plans, a price with currency and interval for each currency sold, feature keys, limits, provider price ids per environment, coupons with an expiry or redemption cap and the plans they apply to, and legacy plan keys with effective dates. Copy, entitlements, limits, and checkout import it; no second definition exists. A parity check proves each configured provider price exists in the catalog with the same amount, currency, and interval, read from the provider's test environment by a script or from a committed export that script refreshes. A recurring discount of 100 percent with no end date is refused. Changing an existing plan keeps the old terms under a legacy key until renewal after a cited notice period, and the notice task precedes the change task.
   Criterion: WHEN plans are planned THE PLAN SHALL name one catalog imported by copy, entitlements, limits, and checkout, with provider parity on id, amount, currency, and interval; IF an existing plan changes THE PLAN SHALL schedule a cited notice first and test that legacy subscribers keep their terms.
8. R-BIZ-8 Displayed price equals charged price. The checkout request carries only a plan key, interval, currency, coupon, and seat count; the server resolves the provider price and amount from the catalog and ignores any amount, price id, or plan in the body. Each market sold states whether displayed prices include tax. One test covers every self-serve plan, interval, currency, and coupon combination at seat counts 1, 2, and each tier's limit, including the shown variant of any pricing experiment, comparing pre-tax amount, currency, interval, and seats and asserting the tax label; another sends a tampered amount, price id, or plan and is charged the catalog amount or refused. A quote-only tier has no displayed price and is covered by R-BIZ-19 override records.
   Criterion: WHEN a pricing surface or checkout is planned THE PLAN SHALL resolve the charged price on the server from the catalog and SHALL contain the sold-combination comparison test and the tampered-request test.
9. R-BIZ-9 Entitlement map, scope, and safe default. One function maps every catalog price id (monthly, annual, legacy, regional, trial) to a plan key and every provider subscription status named in a dated source (for Stripe: incomplete, incomplete_expired, trialing, active, past_due, canceled, unpaid, paused) to an access state. Entitlements derive from the paying unit's entitlement source (decision 4), so a team plan bought by one member reaches every member and an add-on unlocks only the paying unit that bought it. Web and store purchases resolve to one entitlement set per paying unit, with store receipts verified on the server. A null or missing plan resolves to the free plan; an unknown price id resolves to free and raises an alert naming the id; nothing falls through to paid access. Tests run every catalog price id through the purchase event for the buyer and a second member, and cover each mapped status, a new sign-up, a null plan, and an unknown price id.
   Criterion: WHEN entitlements are planned THE PLAN SHALL name one mapping function keyed to the paying unit over every price id and provider status, with a free default, an alert on unknown price ids, and the table-driven tests.
10. R-BIZ-10 Server-enforced gates and sold limits. One server helper checks each paid feature and limit and is called by every route, job, import, and API mount that serves it, listed in the plan; the UI check is display only. The helper reads the paying unit's entitlement source on every check (the webhook-written projection under decision 4 option a, the provider through its short-lived cache under option c), never a session or token claim, so a plan change applies on the next request, or within the cache lifetime under option c. A quota counts the unit and scope the copy sells, resets on the subscription period, and is moved by every consuming path; jobs serving a paid configuration recheck the plan each run. A refusal names the plan that lifts the limit and the upgrade entry point, and tells a member without billing rights who holds them. Tests cover, per plan, use up to the sold limit succeeding and the next unit refused; each consuming path moving the one counter; a period boundary restoring the quota; a free account receiving 402 or 403 with no side effect and the upgrade target in the body; and a job skipped after downgrade.
    Criterion: WHEN a paid feature or limit is planned THE PLAN SHALL name the server helper, every call site, the counted unit and reset period, and the five tests, plus a check that fails when a listed call site skips the helper.

Billing and subscription lifecycle

11. R-BIZ-11 Checkout: access on confirmed payment, live mode, and tax. Self-serve paid access follows the provider's confirmed payment: under decision 4 option a it is granted by the provider's confirmed payment events, including the delayed-payment success event, keyed by the provider's customer and subscription ids, and under option c it is read live from the provider. The success page shows status and may also call an idempotent server function that retrieves the session and checks it is paid and belongs to the account, never as the only grant path. Production startup and the production client build fail on a test-mode key, a sandbox host, or a sandbox client environment. A tier sold on invoice terms grants access only through an R-BIZ-19 override record that references a signed order and carries a payment-due date; an invoice unpaid past that date enters the R-BIZ-14 failed-payment path. When the billing provider is not the merchant of record, checkout enables the provider's automatic tax or passes tax rates, plans sold to businesses collect a tax ID and issue invoices, and tax registration is an owned Open Question. Tests open the success URL with a forged session id and grant nothing, and start the server with production settings and a test key and refuse to start.
    Criterion: IF the plan has a checkout THE PLAN SHALL grant self-serve access only from confirmed payment (the confirmed-payment handler under decision 4 option a, the live provider read under option c), fail production server and client builds on test or sandbox configuration, name the tax mechanism when the provider is not the merchant of record, and contain the forged-session and test-key tests; IF a tier is sold on invoice terms THE PLAN SHALL grant it only through an override record with a payment-due date.
12. R-BIZ-12 Lifecycle event matrix and reconciliation. A table names, in the provider's own event names from a dated source, every lifecycle event the integration handles and the state each writes: created; updated, including plan changes made in the provider's customer portal; paused and resumed when pause is sold; ended; payment failed; payment recovered; refund; dispute; and store notifications for native apps (Paddle, for example, delivers refunds and chargebacks as adjustment events). The refund row states the decision 5 refund policy and revokes what the purchase granted (license keys, credits, seats, access). Each handler applies an event only when it is newer than the stored state, by event timestamp or a re-fetch. A scheduled reconciliation job corrects drifted subscription state, seat quantity, and recorded usage, unless entitlements read the provider live (decision 4 option c), in which case a test proves no entitlement path reads a local plan field. A task confirms the provider endpoint subscribes to every handled event. Signatures stay with R-SEC-19, idempotency with R-DB-9, and amount reconciliation with R-DB-23.
    Criterion: IF the plan uses a billing provider THE PLAN SHALL contain the provider-named event table, a replay test per event, the out-of-order and refund-revocation tests, the endpoint subscription check, and either a reconciliation job or the live-read test.
13. R-BIZ-13 One subscription, both sides, right time. Subscription changes go through one function that reuses the paying unit's provider customer, refuses a second active subscription across web and store billing, cancels every add-on and seat on cancellation or account deletion, keeps access until the paid period ends, replaces rather than stacks on upgrade, and changes provider and app state together. The plan states when an upgrade, a downgrade, and an interval switch take effect and how each is prorated, citing the provider's proration setting, and an upgrade grants the higher plan only after the provider confirms the change and its prorated charge. Mocked-provider tests cover checkout twice, cancel, delete account, downgrade, upgrade, a declined upgrade charge leaving the old plan, and a store subscriber starting web checkout.
    Criterion: IF subscriptions can change THE PLAN SHALL route every change through one named function, state effective timing and proration per change type, and contain the seven mocked-provider tests.
14. R-BIZ-14 Trial, grace, failed payment, and lifecycle notices. Trial end is enforced in the entitlement helper or a registered job, and the expired state keeps a reachable upgrade action. When serving the product costs money per use, the plan states a trial eligibility rule (one trial per paying unit, payment method, or verified domain). A failed payment keeps access through a grace period that is cited or an Open Question, shows an update-payment link, and ends access on the provider's final state; the plan names which provider state ends access after the last retry (for Stripe a dashboard setting) and schedules a task that sets it and records the value. Senders are wired for the trial-ending, renewal, payment-failed, and cancellation notices, with legal minimums cited from a dated source and each regime named in the R-PRD-7 statement of applicable regulations. Tests cover a trial that ended yesterday, failed then paid, and failed then canceled.
    Criterion: IF the plan has a trial or a recurring charge THE PLAN SHALL enforce trial end, a sourced grace period with an update-payment path, the provider's final state, and wired notices with cited minimums, and SHALL contain the three tests.
15. R-BIZ-15 Billed seats and usage match use. Every membership path (settings, invite acceptance, SCIM, admin action, API) updates the billed quantity through one function; a contract that commits a seat count states whether added seats bill immediately, at a scheduled true-up, or at renewal, and the function applies that rule. Usage is recorded on the server after the operation succeeds, once, with a stable identifier the provider deduplicates, and failed operations refund consumed credits. On usage-based plans customers see current-period usage and the projected charge, and the plan decides whether a spend cap or usage alert exists; a cap or alert in copy is an R-BIZ-5 claim. A test drives each membership and usage path.
    Criterion: IF billing is per seat or per unit of usage THE PLAN SHALL name the single quantity-update function and the server-side usage recorder, state the committed-seat rule when contracts commit seats, and test every membership and usage path.
16. R-BIZ-16 Data and members survive downgrade, lapse, and trial end. Over-limit data becomes read-only and exportable. When the lower plan allows fewer seats than are active, the owner chooses who keeps access, every other member is read-only until then, suspended members can sign in and export their own work, and no member or authored record is deleted. The owner notice carries an export link. Deletion happens only after a cited window, in a job that rechecks the plan before deleting, and a customer who resubscribes inside the window returns to the same paying unit with its data. A test moves a five-seat workspace with over-limit records to a one-seat plan and finds every member and record intact, read-only, and exportable, with the owner notice queued.
    Criterion: IF the plan has a paid plan, a trial, or plan limits THE PLAN SHALL keep over-limit data and members read-only rather than deleted, delete only after a sourced window in a plan-rechecking job, and contain the intact-workspace test.

Customer accounts and operations

17. R-BIZ-17 Shared work, ownership, and transfer. The workspace owns shared content and the creator is an attribute, an input to R-DB-6. Removing a member or deleting a user reassigns their shared work instead of deleting it. The last owner cannot be removed, demoted, or deleted until another member is promoted, and a transfer-ownership action exists for the owner and for support.
    Criterion: IF workspaces have more than one member THE PLAN SHALL assign shared content to the workspace, reassign on member removal, block last-owner removal, and plan a transfer action, each with a test.
18. R-BIZ-18 Closure stops everything; export covers everything. Closing an account or workspace stops every integration, webhook, schedule, and outbound send in one recorded job. When export is promised or a plan is sold to businesses, export covers every content type customers create in an open, re-importable format, and each per-type test creates more records than one page holds. A data processing service sold to customers in the EU names EU Data Act Chapter VI as a regime its signal may trigger, and its export and closure tasks cite the switching windows from a dated source (notice at most 2 months, transition at most 30 calendar days, at least 30 days to retrieve data, no switching charges from 12 January 2027) or an Open Question.
    Criterion: IF the plan has customer accounts THE PLAN SHALL stop every scheduled effect on closure in one recorded job with a test that nothing remains scheduled; IF export is promised or sold to businesses THE PLAN SHALL export every content type and test each beyond one page.
19. R-BIZ-19 Account operations as product actions; deals as records. Recurring support operations (plan change, trial extension, credit, refund, ownership transfer, account restore, account email change, account merge) are role-restricted admin actions built on the lifecycle functions, mounted in the production build behind the support role, calling the provider and taking a reason field, never database edits. Customer-specific arrangements and invoice-term tiers are override records (plan, limits, features, deal reference, payment-due date, end date), never branches on one customer's id, domain, or email.
    Criterion: IF the plan has paying customers at funded-product or enterprise scale, admin tooling, invoice-term tiers, or custom deals THE PLAN SHALL plan each recurring support operation as a production-mounted admin action with a reason field, and SHALL store deals as override records.

Product metrics and instrumentation

20. R-BIZ-20 Typed tracking plan and outcome events. One typed event catalog names every event in object-action form; CI compares the names the code sends against it and fails on any analytics SDK call outside the tracking wrapper. Outcome events (the core value action, activation, sign-up, purchase) fire once, server-side after commit or in the confirmed webhook, with a dedup id the analytics tool honors. Revenue and subscription events come from the billing webhook with amount, currency, plan, interval, and account id. Tests assert a failed request sends no outcome event, a successful one sends exactly one, and replayed payment, renewal, refund, and cancellation webhooks each send one event with those fields.
    Criterion: IF analytics events are planned THE PLAN SHALL name the typed catalog and its CI check, send outcome and revenue events server-side with dedup ids, and contain the send-once and webhook-replay tests.
21. R-BIZ-21 Identity, consent, and environment separation. The tracking wrapper identifies with the stable internal user id after sign-up and sign-in, groups by the paying unit, and resets on sign-out. It sends nothing for a user or account that rejected tracking, on the server path as well as the client path (R-SEC-29), and event properties carry ids, plan, platform, and amounts, never email, name, or free text. Sign-up stores the first-touch acquisition source (R-LAUNCH-16 UTM values) on the account. Each environment has its own analytics project key set by deploy configuration, tests and CI stub the client, a production-configuration test asserts the wrapper is enabled, and internal, test, and demo accounts carry a property every metric filters on.
    Criterion: IF analytics events are planned THE PLAN SHALL specify identify, group, and reset points, a server-side opt-out, a property allowlist, per-environment keys, a stubbed client in tests, the production-enabled test, and the internal-account marker.

Business metric definitions

22. R-BIZ-22 Business metrics defined once, as labeled. Each business metric the plan computes has one definition (a function, view, or semantic-layer metric) that every report reads. MRR normalizes each subscription's price times quantity to a month for the paying statuses the definition lists by name, net of recurring discounts, excluding tax, refunds, one-time charges, and trials. Amounts in different currencies are grouped per currency or converted at a recorded rate. The real-customer filter excludes internal, test, and demo accounts. Active means distinct users who performed a named key action in the window. Churn divides by the count at the start of the period, revenue churn includes contraction, a conversion rate takes numerator and denominator from one population, and closed accounts stay in historical cohorts as non-personal records. Windows are half-open in one stated time zone and match their labels.
    Criterion: IF the plan computes MRR, active users, churn, retention, or conversion THE PLAN SHALL name one definition per metric that every report reads, applying each R-BIZ-22 rule, and SHALL contain a fixture test per rule.

Release, rollout, and sunset

23. R-BIZ-23 Release flags and kill switches that work. One typed flag wrapper is the only way code reads a flag; when the flag service cannot answer it falls back to current production behavior and alerts. Keys are generated from the flag definitions, each with an owner and an expiry date, and unreleased features are evaluated on the server for every route they use. Features that move money, send customer messages, or delete or export customer data get a kill switch read per request and per job run on every path of the feature (routes, jobs, webhooks). Percentage rollouts bucket by a deterministic hash of the stable id; targeting rules read only attributes the code sends in the evaluation context, never a hardcoded staff email or id list; a beta or preview names its label beside the feature, its opt-in, and a graduation or removal date; a sold feature names the rule that enables it for the plans that bought it; a mobile app plans a remote minimum-version switch.
    Criterion: IF the plan introduces flags, or at funded-product scale or above ships features that move money, send customer messages, or delete or export customer data, THE PLAN SHALL specify the typed wrapper as the only flag reader with an alerting production-behavior fallback, owned and expiring keys, and server evaluation, and SHALL test that a runtime kill-switch flip stops the next request and job run without a restart.
24. R-BIZ-24 Deprecation, breaking changes, and customer release notes. A deprecated feature, plan, or public API version states its end date, its replacement, a cited notice period, the in-product notice to affected accounts, and the query that counts remaining callers. Deprecated API versions send `Deprecation` as an RFC 9745 date (`@<epoch>`) and `Sunset` as an RFC 8594 HTTP-date no earlier than it; deprecated SDK functions and CLI commands emit a runtime warning naming the end date and replacement. A breaking change to a public contract (API, SDK, webhook payload, CLI, export format) follows R-ARCH-20 for APIs and a SemVer major (R-REPO-13) for packages; this requirement adds the migration note and a contract test pinning the old shape, and removing the retired mount stays with R-SEC-19. Customer release notes and in-app what's-new entries are separate from the developer changelog (R-REPO-8) and generated platform notes (R-REPO-13), and each entry is published only after the task that enables the feature for its audience.
    Criterion: IF the plan deprecates a feature, plan, or public API version THE PLAN SHALL state the end date, replacement, sourced notice period, in-product notice, caller-count query, and for API versions both headers; IF it breaks a public contract THE PLAN SHALL add a migration note and an old-shape contract test; WHEN customer release notes are planned THE PLAN SHALL gate each entry on its enabling task.

Experimentation

25. R-BIZ-25 Experiments that can decide. Each experiment assigns variants by a deterministic hash of the stable id at the paying-unit level when price or shared flows change, logs exposure once where the variant renders, applies eligibility before assignment with weights summing to the allocation, sends every targeting attribute in the evaluation context, and names a layer or mutual-exclusion rule with any experiment that changes the same surface. It records its end date, the sample-size source, the primary metric and guardrail from the register, the decision rule, and a task that removes the losing branch.
    Criterion: IF the plan runs a controlled experiment THE PLAN SHALL specify each R-BIZ-25 design field and schedule the cleanup task.

Customer feedback and demand signals

26. R-BIZ-26 Customer signals reach a person with context. Each feedback, support, survey, bug-report, cancellation-reason, contact-sales, or demand surface (notify-me, request access, feature request, and any waitlist beyond R-LAUNCH-12) names its destination per environment and one reader, with a production startup check that rejects placeholder destinations; the R-PRD-17 runbook task links the readers. Submissions carry user, account, plan, page, and app version from the session, not from the form; demand signals also carry the item requested, and survey responses store the question and scale. Cancellation reasons are stored with account, plan, tenure, and date where churn is reviewed. Ratings on model output stay with R-LLM-19.
    Criterion: WHEN a customer-signal surface is planned THE PLAN SHALL name its per-environment destination and reader, a placeholder-rejecting startup check, session-sourced context, and a delivery test.

## Self-audit rubric

100 points total. Below 85 total, revise before emission. A dimension whose requirements are all dropped leaves the denominator, and the rest re-normalize to 100.

Floor: when the plan charges money, R-BIZ-7 through R-BIZ-16 each bind wherever their own condition holds. Only an archetype-layer drop that cites the decision removing the condition counts; any other disposition, a scale-layer drop included, caps the module at 69. R-BIZ-5, R-BIZ-6, and R-BIZ-17 hold the same floor wherever their conditions hold, charging or not. Together they design out productauditor's five Critical classes, and a plan that would charge customers wrongly, refuse what they paid for, run a trial nothing ends, sell what does not exist, or lose their data cannot grade well however good the rest is.

## Anti-patterns refused

- Strategy theater: a vision paragraph and a bet with no kill criterion, decide-by point, or decider, or a verdict an executing agent fills in after launch. Refusal: R-BIZ-1.
- Principles nobody pays for: "simple, fast, delightful", or "reliability over outages", where no product would choose the other side. Refusal: R-BIZ-4.
- Invented price: a price point chosen at plan time with no source, user answer, decision, or validation task. Refusal: R-BIZ-2.
- Fiction pricing page: copy that lists features or limits no entitlement code reads. Refusal: R-BIZ-5 and R-BIZ-7.
- Display-only currency: a currency switcher that converts the shown price while checkout charges another currency. Refusal: R-BIZ-7 prices each currency sold.
- Paper paywall: a plan-gate helper never mounted on the API, or a check that runs only in the client. Refusal: R-BIZ-10 names and checks every call site.
- Fall-through to paid: an unknown or null plan treated as the top tier. Refusal: R-BIZ-9.
- Success-redirect grant: paid access set on the checkout success page or from a query parameter. Refusal: R-BIZ-11.
- Checkout-only webhook: a handler that switches only on checkout completion and logs everything else. Refusal: R-BIZ-12.
- Local-only cancel: a cancel button that flips local state and never calls the provider, so the customer keeps paying. Refusal: R-BIZ-13.
- The trial that never ends: a `trial_ends_at` written at sign-up and read only by a countdown banner. Refusal: R-BIZ-14.
- Downgrade that deletes: over-limit records or members removed when a plan lapses. Refusal: R-BIZ-16.
- KPI theater: a north star or MRR figure with no register entry, definition, or source event. Refusal: R-BIZ-3 and R-BIZ-22.
- Client-only revenue: purchase events sent from the browser after the redirect. Refusal: R-BIZ-20.
- A flag that fails open, or a kill switch read once at start: nothing stops at the moment it is needed. Refusal: R-BIZ-23.
- Deprecation with no date: an endpoint marked deprecated forever. Refusal: R-BIZ-24.
- Feedback to nowhere: a feedback widget or contact form posting to a placeholder or to a route nobody reads. Refusal: R-BIZ-26.
